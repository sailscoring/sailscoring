# Champions' Cup format survey — elimination to a final series

A companion to `format-survey.md`, which surveyed the premier championships of
twelve Olympic and youth classes and found one family (Appendix LE Addendum C)
behind almost all of them. This survey covers a format that is **not** in that
family: Irish Sailing's Champions' Cup events, where a fleet is divided not to
make it manageable but to eliminate most of it.

Primary sources are the 2025 Notices of Race and Sailing Instructions for all
three events, captured with the published results in
`reference-docs:events/irish-champions-cup-{keelboat,dinghy,junior}-2025/`.

## The format, as the SIs write it

The Keelboat and Dinghy events run the same three-stage structure. Quoting the
keelboat SIs (the dinghy text is identical bar the boat and the repêchage
shape):

> **6.2** The competition will consist of a Qualifying Series (sailed in 2
> fleets) and a Repêchage if possible, to select the helms that qualify to sail
> in a Final Series. Boat draw will take place at the briefing.
>
> **6.3** Helms ranked 1st and 2nd from each Qualifying Fleet will compete in
> the Final Series.
>
> **6.4** Helms ranked from 3rd to 5th from each Qualifying Fleet may sail a
> Repêchage of up to 2 races. Scoring for this round shall not include
> preliminary race results. If the repêchage rounds cannot be sailed, and there
> are available boats, the organising committee may invite the 3rd placed
> sailors from the qualifying flights to compete in the final.
>
> **6.5** Helms ranked 1st and 2nd in the Repêchage may qualify for the Final
> Series.

and the whole of its scoring:

> **17.1** One Final Series race is required to be completed to constitute a
> series.
> **17.2** A Helm's series score will be the total of their race scores in the
> Final Series only. No scores shall be excluded.
> **17.3** The Qualifying Series and Repêchage are solely to qualify the helms
> for the Final Series. No scores shall be excluded. No scores will be carried
> forward to the Final Series.
> **17.4** Rule A5.3 applies.

Twelve helms are invited, drawn at random into two flights of six on Saturday
and sailing up to three races each. Four advance directly, two more through the
repêchage, and the six-boat Final Series of up to four races on Sunday decides
the championship on its own. Boats are supplied — J/80s at the Royal Irish,
National 18s at Royal Cork — and redrawn between stages, so a boat draw stands
where a seeding committee would in an LE event.

The Junior Champions' Cup is a different animal despite sharing the branding
and the SI heading "QUALIFICATION FOR THE FINAL": sixteen helms, one opening
series of up to eight races with one discard from five races, and a ten-boat
medal race at double Appendix A4 points that may not be excluded (NoR 15.1–15.3)
— that is `format-survey.md`'s **F2**, already covered, and it needs nothing new.

All three events are umpired under World Sailing Appendix UF, whose penalties
are scoring penalties in points: 1 point for contact between hulls, 2 or more
where damage results, and a protest committee free to award "points or fraction
of points" (SI Addendum C, UF1.3 and UF5.8(d)). The keelboat SIs add a damage
table with 2-point and 4-point tiers.

## What separates it from Appendix LE

LE Addendum C 7.4(a) assigns **every** boat to a final-series fleet: the split
is a banding of the whole entry into Gold, Silver, Bronze, and every race a boat
sails counts toward one championship score. LE contemplates a cut in exactly one
place — the medal race of 7.5 — and even there the boats who miss it are
bracketed as sailing one more race.

The Champions' Cup inverts every part of that.

| | Appendix LE Addendum C | Champions' Cup |
|---|---|---|
| Why the fleet is split | too many boats for one start | only six boats exist |
| Who continues | everyone, banded | four of twelve, plus two from the repêchage |
| Qualifying ranking | one cross-fleet list | per-flight, never merged |
| Reassignment between rounds | nightly, by rank | none — flights are drawn once |
| Carry into the deciding stage | continuous points (or net, or rank) | **nothing** |
| Discards | a ladder by race count | none, at any stage |
| A5.2 replacement base | largest fleet | A5.3, "came to the starting area" |
| Second chance for the eliminated | none | the repêchage |

"Repêchage" appears nowhere in the RRS, nowhere in either edition of Appendix
LE, and nowhere in the Sailwave or YNZ material on running flighted events. It
is organiser vocabulary borrowed from rowing and judo, and it names a stage the
rules have no word for.

Two of these differences are more than vocabulary:

- **The per-flight quota makes cross-flight equalisation meaningless.** LE's
  validity gate (a qualifying race counts only once every fleet has sailed it)
  exists because the qualifying ranking is a single cross-fleet list, so
  unequal race counts would corrupt it. Here each flight is an independent
  selection pool whose only output is its own top two, so a flight that sails
  three races while the other sails one has produced a perfectly good answer.
  Applying LE's gate would throw away racing that decided the event.
- **A5.3 is the right base and the fleet size is not.** The SIs invoke A5.3, so
  a boat's DNS/OCS/DSQ score is one more than the number of boats that came to
  the starting area of that race — not one more than the fleet's assigned size,
  and not one more than the twelve entered. In the 2025 dinghy qualifying, John
  O'Driscoll's OCS in Q2 of the A flight scored **7.0** in a six-boat flight of
  a twelve-boat entry. (Scoring each flight as its own six-boat series gives the
  same 7.0, which is in fact how the results were published — the two readings
  are indistinguishable here, which is itself the point: the base tracks the
  stage, not the entry list.)

## What actually happened in 2025

The 2025 events are the first running of the split Dinghy/Keelboat format;
Irish Sailing separated the two after 78 years of a single Champions' Cup (the
"All Ireland Sailing Championships", earlier the "Helmsman's Championships").
The qualifying-flights-plus-repêchage structure long predates the split: earlier
reporting of the All Irelands describes "two flights of eight boats… three races
apiece", and a year in which six sailors went through to the final with two
repêchage places still to come. So the quotas move year to year — three per
flight plus two then, two per flight plus two now — while the shape holds.

**Dinghy, Royal Cork, 4–5 October** — two flights of six, three races each, then
a six-boat final of four races. Published as *two separate Sailwave files*: a
qualifying file with the two flights as fleets A and B, and a `… 2025 F.htm`
final-series file with six entries. The scorer never produced a combined
ranking, because the SIs do not define one. Ger Owens (GP14) won on an
A8.1 tie-break from a **three-way tie at 12.0 points** with Alex Barry (National
18) and Josh Porter (Fireball) — 1,6,4,1 against 4,5,1,2 against 2,3,2,5, split
by best-to-worst score lists alone.

**Keelboat, Royal Irish, 11–12 October** — light easterlies cut Saturday to two
races in flight A and none in flight B, with one race abandoned on a shift. The
SIs were amended overnight to let the final start after the 1500 cut-off ("SI
6.12 … unless it is the first race of the final series"). Cillian Dickson won
from David Johnston and Fionn Lyden. Irish Sailing's HalSail account carries the
event as three separate series — Qualifying Series A, Qualifying Series B and
Champions Cup Final — behind a viewer that cannot be captured statically.

**Neither event sailed the repêchage.** Both fell back to SI 6.4's last
sentence and invited the third-placed helm from each flight, giving a
2+2+2-from-two-flights final in both cases. The fallback is not an edge case;
it is what happened in the first year of the format, at both events.

**Junior, Fastnet Marine, 20–21 September** — sixteen boats, six opening races
with one discard, ten-boat medal race. The six boats who missed the cut were
scored **DNC 34.0** in the medal race: A5.3's second limb gives 17 (entries
plus one) and the double-points instruction doubles it. Daniel Copithorne won.

## Is this format used outside Irish Sailing?

Largely, no — and where a national champion-of-champions exists elsewhere, it
is usually a plain series.

- **US Sailing Championship of Champions** — up to 20 invited champions in
  supplied boats. Its Conditions of Race are explicit: "The Low Point Scoring
  System (RRS Appendix A) will be used, except that if only seven races are
  completed, no race scores will be excluded" (9.1), with "a complete round
  robin… desired… a minimum rotation of two boat swaps per day" (7.4). One
  fleet, one series, boat rotation instead of flights. No split, no cut, no
  repêchage.
- **Endeavour Trophy** — the UK's champion-of-champions event, run by the
  Royal Corinthian YC at Burnham for invited national champions in supplied
  boats (Melges 15s since 2025). A single fleet sailing a straight series;
  2025 finished on four races counting with no discard. No split, no final.
- **SAILING Champions League** and the national leagues that feed it come
  closest structurally, but from the other direction. At the 2025 Final, 33
  club teams sailed short races in supplied boats to a published pairing list,
  eleven teams to a flight, each team completing 14–16 races. That is a
  rotation device — a cousin of the Topper Worlds' frozen flights and fixed
  pairing rota (`format-survey.md`'s **F8**) — rather than an elimination. The
  SIs published for the Final are procedural only; the format itself lives in
  the league's own rules, which this survey did not retrieve.
- **Knockout finales** in the Olympic windsurf and kite disciplines
  (`format-survey.md`'s **F4**) do eliminate, but by bracket rather than by a
  scored final series, and the RRS gives them their own machinery — Appendix B's
  A5.4 and Appendix F's A5.2 both set the code base to the size of the heat, the
  same instinct as A5.3 here.
- Club-level champion-of-champions events with elimination rounds exist (the
  CYCA's Elliott 7m event has been run as a round robin whittling to a
  best-of-three final in some years), but they are one-off local formats rather
  than a documented family.

So the honest position is that this is an **Irish national-authority format**,
run three times a year at events Sail Scoring is plausibly asked to score,
rather than an international one. Its value is not breadth of adoption; it is
that Irish Sailing is the national authority, the events are currently split
across Sailwave and HalSail with no combined result, and the format exercises
primitives (elimination cuts, zero carry, stage-scoped code bases) that
`format-survey.md` already identified as gaps.

## Proposed fixture code

**F9 — elimination to a final series.** Qualifying flights drawn once and never
re-dealt; advancement by a per-flight quota rather than an overall ranking; an
optional intermediate stage (the repêchage) scored on its own and promoting a
further quota; a deciding stage that carries nothing forward, has no discards
and is the entire championship score; A5.3 as the code base throughout.

Scenario fixtures over F9, all drawn from 2025:

| # | Scenario | Real instance |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Repêchage not sailed** — the quota widens (2nd → 3rd per flight) and the final is filled from qualifying alone | both 2025 events |
| E2 | **Flights sail unequal numbers of races** — each flight's own ranking still selects; no equalisation | keelboat 2025 (A 2 races, B 0 on day one) |
| E3 | **Final decided on an A8.1 tie** at equal points, no discards to fall back on | dinghy 2025, three-way tie at 12.0 |
| E4 | **One final race constitutes a series** (SI 17.1) — a championship on a single race, no discard | not yet sailed; SI-mandated |
| E5 | **Eliminated boats have no championship score** — the published result is two stage tables, not one ranking | both 2025 events, as published |
| E6 | **Appendix UF points penalties** — 1, 2 and 4-point umpire-imposed scoring penalties and fractional protest-committee awards | SI Addendum C/D, all three events |

## How this could be scored

### The directional question

The feature currently models an Appendix LE championship: three stages named
`qualifying` / `final` / `medal`, fleets banded so everyone continues, a
continuous (or net, or rank-seeded) carry, and a discard ladder. The question is
whether the Champions' Cup family belongs inside that or beside it.

**Recommendation: inside it, by generalising four policies that are currently
hard-wired to Appendix LE's answers — not by adding a second feature.**

The reason is that the *data model* already fits and the *scoring policies* do
not. Rounds of real fleets, per-fleet stage races, one interleaved finish sheet
with cross-fleet `place` and within-fleet `rank`, assignment lists published as
permanent artifacts, stage-scoped standings, per-stage code bases, and a carry
hook that supersedes earlier cells with a single carried number — all of that is
built, and the Champions' Cup needs every part of it. The 2025 dinghy qualifying
results are literally an interleaved two-flight sheet (the B flight's winner is
recorded at raw place 7, scoring 1.0), which is the `finishSheets: 'combined'`
case the engine already handles. A separate feature would rebuild rounds, fleet
assignment, stage standings, publishing, the file format and the e2e surface for
a twelve-boat weekend event.

It also fits the shape of the existing design. The medal stage already **is** an
elimination cut with a self-contained score, and the vocabulary machinery exists
precisely so that stage names never leak into what a scorer reads — this is the
case it was designed for. And one of the four policies below (`carry: 'none'`)
is already on the roadmap as F7, the current Topper practice.

What would be wrong is to keep calling the feature "Appendix LE support". It is
better understood as: *a championship sailed in stages, where each stage has its
own fleets and its own relationship to the championship score.* Appendix LE is
one preset over that; the Champions' Cup is another.

### The four policies to generalise

1. **How a stage's membership is decided.** Today: band everyone (the split
   ceremony) or take the top N overall (the medal ceremony). Add a per-flight
   quota — "top k from each fleet of the previous stage" — with k adjustable at
   ceremony time, which is exactly what E1's fallback needs, and manual
   promotion for the repêchage graduates.
2. **What earlier stages contribute.** Add `carry: 'none'` alongside `points`,
   `net-plus-net` and `rank-seed`. The mechanism exists: `carryTransform`
   already supersedes a boat's opening cells and replaces them with one carried
   number, so carrying nothing is that path with no replacement cell. Boats who
   never made the cut keep their own stage's cells and rank within it, which is
   the two-tables-not-one-ranking outcome E5 asks for.
3. **Whether cross-fleet equalisation applies.** Add `equalization: 'none'` for
   the case where the flights are independent selection pools. Without it, E2
   scores the keelboat event wrongly — LE's validity gate would void flight A's
   two races because flight B had sailed none.
4. **The A5.2 replacement base.** Add A5.3 as a first-class option per stage:
   one more than the boats that came to the starting area, falling back to
   entries-plus-one for a boat that never came. This is what both Irish SIs
   invoke, and it is the honest base for any stage whose fleet size differs from
   its starting-area count.

Plus, smaller: a third vocabulary set (qualifying series / repêchage / final
series), and — for the Junior event — a `companionRace: 'dnc'` option where the
boats who miss the cut are scored DNC in the deciding race rather than sailing
one of their own, and the medal multiplier applied to code scores as well as
place scores, which is what produced the published 34.0.

### Mapping onto the existing three stages

The stored stage enum does not change, so there is no migration and no new
stage on race starts or split rounds. The four policies widen `qf_config`,
which the series file and the public export carry, so those need the usual
round-trip check. The stages map as:

| SI stage | Engine stage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Qualifying Series (2 flights) | `qualifying` | drawn once, no rank-pattern reassignment |
| Repêchage | `final` | one or two fleets holding only the 3rd–5th placed; scored standalone; empty when not sailed |
| Final Series | `medal` | one fleet, `multiplier: 1`, non-discardable, carry `none` — the whole championship score |

The middle slot is empty whenever the repêchage is not sailed, which is what
happened at both 2025 events — and the deciding stage's ceremony does not
support that today. It is handed the final round and selects the top *n* rows of
that round's first fleet, so it needs a split round to exist and can only cut
from one fleet. Widening it to "top *k* from each fleet of the previous stage,
whichever stage that is" is the same change as policy 1 and covers both the
repêchage-sailed and repêchage-not-sailed paths.

This mapping runs out if an event ever wants a repêchage **and** a medal race —
four stages. That is the trigger to make the stage list data rather than a fixed
enum, and it is a migration (the enum is stored on race starts, split rounds,
the series file and the public export), so it should wait for a real event that
needs it rather than being done speculatively.

### What not to build

The older All-Ireland formats and the club-level variants that run a round robin
whittling down to a best-of-three shoot-out are brackets, and the same argument
that keeps F4 out of the engine keeps them out: record the outcome as stated
points with a manual rank override, do not compute the bracket. Appendix UF's
points penalties need nothing new — an umpire's 1-, 2- or 4-point scoring
penalty is a DPI with an explicit points override, and fractional protest
committee awards are already representable.
